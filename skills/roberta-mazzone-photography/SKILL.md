# Roberta Mazzone Photography Guide

Use this skill as an independent, descriptive guide derived from publicly available work and interviews. Do not present it as official, endorsed, authorized, or capable of reproducing any creator exactly.

## Ground rules

- Translate the named reference into general visual traits before producing creative work.
- Do not put `Roberta Mazzone`, `_lagiuditta`, or any living creator's name in a final image-generation prompt. Use descriptive language instead.
- Do not promise an exact replica or copy a recognizable published frame. Change the location, action, wardrobe, spatial design, and composition enough to make the result original.
- Distinguish documented statements from visual inference. Never invent a camera body, lens, preset, film stock, or fixed color recipe.
- Keep public reference images out of deliverables unless the user owns or licenses them. Link to sources instead.
- Protect people and places: obtain consent where appropriate, avoid exposing fragile locations, and remove precise geotags when disclosure could cause harm.
- Answer in the user's language. For image generators, default to an English production prompt plus notes in the user's language unless asked otherwise.

## Core workflow

1. **Classify the task.** Select `shoot`, `generate`, `edit`, `critique`, or `story-sequence`. Combine modes only when the brief requires it.
2. **Normalize the brief.** Extract subject, place, emotional beat, intended use, aspect ratio, number of frames, season, time of day, people, wardrobe, props, and practical limits. Make modest defaults when details are missing.
3. **Write one mood sentence.** Describe what the place should feel like, not merely what it contains. Example: `A still, sun-warmed pause between indoors and the sea.`
4. **Choose four to six DNA pillars.** Select only the traits that serve the story. Favor place, light, composition, human state, material, and color over decorative keywords.
5. **Build a visual arc.** Use at least three distinct beats: context, lived moment, and residue/detail. For an eight-frame story, default to two establishing frames, two threshold or architectural frames, two human moments, one material detail, and one quiet closing frame.
6. **Execute the relevant branch.** Follow the shooting or generation reference. Keep the environment as a co-author rather than a passive backdrop.
7. **Run the quality gate.** Score the result with the checklist, fix the three largest deviations, and check hard failures.
8. **Deliver decisions, not a mood-board dump.** Explain the selected DNA, the practical output, and what to avoid.

## Default assumptions

Use these only when the user gives no contrary direction:

- Create an eight-frame editorial story.
- Prefer 4:5 vertical for social/editorial portraits and 3:2 horizontal for landscape or interiors.
- Work near early morning, late afternoon, sunset, or directional window light.
- Keep one place-specific accent and an otherwise warm, restrained palette.
- Treat grain, haze, flare, and motion blur as optional accents, never mandatory style tokens.
- Favor a normal-to-short-telephoto perspective; avoid ultrawide distortion unless architecture requires it.

## Output contract

For a shoot plan, return:

1. Story intent and mood sentence
2. Selected DNA pillars
3. Location and light plan
4. Wardrobe, props, and subject direction
5. Numbered shot list with framing, action, and technical starting points
6. Editing direction
7. Avoid list and quality scorecard

For image generation, return:

1. Story intent and selected DNA pillars
2. One production-ready prompt per requested frame
3. A concise negative prompt or positive constraints, depending on the generator
4. Aspect-ratio and variation guidance
5. Originality changes made relative to any references
6. Quality scorecard

For critique, return:

1. What already works
2. The three largest mismatches
3. Specific crop, light, color, direction, or prompt revisions
4. Before/after prompt wording when relevant
5. Checklist score and pass/revise verdict

## Evidence boundary

The public record supports an architectural background, slow-travel and lifestyle subject matter, natural-light observation, environment-led composition, manual Fuji/RAW capture, a stated preference for f/2.8 or f/4, and a slight warm shift. Focal lengths, exact film simulations, fixed grain levels, preset curves, and universal ratios are not documented; treat them as task-specific suggestions or visual inferences.
